# S.T.A.L.K.E.R.: Зов Припяти 98 — Затон

What *Call of Pripyat* could have looked like if GSC had shipped it in 1999: a software-rendered open-world shooter/RPG covering the whole of Zaton. The game is written from scratch. It shares no code, graphics or mechanics with `stalker98` (the WebGL *Shadow of Chernobyl* remake) or with the text quest `stalker.js`.

- Launch from the desktop, the Start menu, the Run command (`zaton`, `cop`, `cop98`, `call of pripyat`) or `C:\Games\CoP98\ZATON.EXE`.
- Run it standalone at `cop98.html`; the page exposes `window.cpui`.
- Run the tests with `npm run test:cop98`, which is `node tests/cop98.test.js` and takes about 7 s.

## Technology (why it looks like 1999)

| Layer | Approach |
|---|---|
| Terrain | Comanche/Delta Force–style **voxel space** raycaster over a 1024×1024 m heightmap (1 m cells). The colour map is baked with slope lighting, sun shadows and AO. Water is drawn as a flat reflective plane at level 0. The camera has real pitch, not a shifted horizon: the raycaster marches vertical planes of the world, and each plane is drawn along a screen line through the vanishing point of vertical lines, so walls converge when you look up or down. Lines fan out from that point, and lines near it are thinned out so pixels are not drawn many times. |
| Structures | Software **triangle rasterizer** with perspective-correct texture mapping, a z-buffer shared with the voxel pass, flat per-face lighting and indoor/outdoor light groups. |
| Characters | **Pre-rendered sprites** drawn as upright cards in 3D, so they foreshorten when you look up or down (small items, artifacts and effects face the camera instead): part rigs (ellipsoids, boxes, cylinders, camouflage and leather materials) are ray-traced into 8 directions × animation frames on demand, then outlined and shade-quantized. Actors closer than 6 m get a second sprite set at double resolution. Mutants have their own rigs: a lean hound with bent hind legs, a lopsided flesh with an eye cluster, a boar with a hump and tusks, a humanoid bloodsucker with mouth tentacles, and a two-headed chimera. |
| Portraits | Every look has a head-and-shoulders bust built from signed distance fields (smooth-blended skull, jaw, cheeks, nose, lips, eyelids, noise-displaced hair and beards, hoods, masks, gas masks, collars) and ray-marched with soft shadows, ambient occlusion and a CRT scanline pass. The five mutants have busts of their own (hide with scars and veins, wet mouths, teeth, glowing eyes). A bust takes about 0.5 s, so the UI renders them in 2–8 ms slices between frames and shows a placeholder until the bust is ready. |
| Items | Inventory icons, ground sprites and first-person weapons share one set of part models. Icons are ray-traced with 2×2 or 3×3 supersampling, bevelled box edges, materials (wood grain, blued metal, bakelite, brass, glass, glowing crystal), a drop shadow and 16-bit dithering, at the pixel size of one backpack cell. |
| Weapons | First-person models are real-time meshes of those part models, drawn by a view-layer rasterizer with its own depth buffer, per-pixel procedural material (wood grain, brushed metal) and dithered shading. The hands find the grip, the forend and the magazine from part roles. |
| Sky | A software sky shader (`cp-shaders`). The sky is evaluated on an 8 px grid of nodes (gradient, sun halo, dusk glow toward the sun, moon halo, cloud-plane coordinates, mip level) and interpolated per pixel. Clouds lie on a flat deck above the camera, so they shrink toward the horizon and drift with the wind. Cumulus comes from a tileable, histogram-equalized noise texture, so the weather's cover value is the share of sky covered. Each cloud pixel takes a second sample offset toward the sun for self-shadowing and gets a silver rim when you look toward the sun. A higher cirrus layer is a stretched noise texture. Mip levels are picked per pixel with Bayer-dithered LOD, and clouds are computed once per 2×2 pixels. 2 600 catalogued stars are projected as points, twinkle and are hidden by clouds. The sun and moon are discs dimmed by the cloud density in their direction; the moon has maria. Lightning is a midpoint-displacement bolt with branches that lights up the clouds around it; during an emission the clouds pulse red. |
| Weather | Clear, overcast, rain and fog (`cp-weather`) blend smoothly: clouds gather first, rain starts only under a closed sky, and the ground keeps its wetness after the rain. Wet ground and structures get darker; on flat roads, mud and concrete, puddles reflect the sky, and rain drops draw animated rings on puddles and water. Rain is drawn as world-space streaks falling with the wind around the camera (depth-tested, so roofs and walls hide them), splashes on the ground and far rain curtains. Fog is exponential height fog integrated along each ray from the depth buffer, with wisps that drift with the wind and sun in-scatter; it pools in lowlands and over water, and hills rise out of it. Heavy rain brings distant lightning with thunder delayed by distance. |
| Shots and light | Tracers are glowing segments that fly along the shot path. Muzzle flashes are procedural stars with 4–6 random petals, stretched along the barrel. Muzzle flashes, explosions and night campfires are point lights applied to the depth buffer, so walls and ground around them light up. Electra discharges are jagged arcs, sparks are velocity streaks, and smoke and dust are soft round particles that fade where they touch geometry. |
| Post | Flashlight cone, sun shafts (radial blur of the bright sky around the sun at quarter resolution), damage vignette, radiation noise, emission tint, weather colour grading (saturation, contrast, tint) and 16-bit colour with ordered dithering (can be switched off). |
| Audio | Every sound is synthesized with WebAudio: 91 buffers including a Karplus–Strong guitar. |

Internal resolution is 200/240/300/360 rows (default 300), with width set by the window's aspect ratio. A frame at 480×300 takes about 10–13 ms in Node: 7–8 ms for terrain, structures, sprites and post, 2–3 ms for the sky, and up to 2 ms more for fog and rain.

## Narrative design

**Premise.** SBU Major Alexander Degtyarev goes into Zaton undercover as a lone stalker. His job is to find out why the five helicopters of Operation "Fairway" went down.

**Theme.** A map is a promise the Zone does not keep. The helicopters died trusting outdated anomaly maps. Degtyarev survives only by trusting the people who live there.

**Pillars.**
1. You are an outsider. Trust (Skadovsk reputation) has to be earned through deeds.
2. The Zone kills the careless, not the weak: anomalies, emissions, radiation and hunger.
3. Every rumour is a lead. Quests come from people and places, not from a quest board.
4. Choices echo. How you get the flight recorder and whom you side with shape the epilogue.
5. No chosen one: Degtyarev is an officer with an AK and a detector.

**Main line (foldback structure).** Reach Skadovsk → Beard → Owl (pay 1500 or bring a Medusa from the Claw) → Skat-2 in the Iron Forest at the old substation (flight engineer's log, the first scripted emission) and Skat-3 on the Southern Plateau (the crew's scratched note, a looter's PDA naming Tesak). The plateau has no way up on foot: the objective first leads to the teleport on the south-eastern slope of the Burnt Farmstead (Owl and Noah tell about it), and a second teleport on the plateau brings the player back. The two crash sites can be done in either order. Then get the flight recorder **four ways** from Tesak's mercenaries in the Substation Workshops: buy it for 8000, clear the Dredge Station of zombies for them, betray Nimble to Sultan (the case is hidden in a container at the Stoyanka), or return Nimble's case and steal the recorder at night. Next: Cardan decodes the recorder for a bottle of vodka → the route to Jupiter from Beard (needs reputation ≥ 2 or a rescued Lotsman) or from Owl for 3000 → a battery from the truck at the substation → the report from the control room of the Iron Forest → a scripted emission, the final chimera and the race into the old railway tunnel behind the substation → epilogue slides.

**Side quests.** Bloodsucker lair under the Krug antenna complex (Beard; at night bloodsuckers hunt around Izumrudnoye until it is cleared), rough tools on the attic of the sawmill office and fine tools on the Dredge Station (Cardan unlocks upgrade tiers 2 and 3), Lotsman's SOS on the Shevchenko (timed; if you rescue him he later escorts you to the tunnel), Noah's dog (Stone Blood from the Oakpine; unlocks the chest, marks the plateau anomalies on the PDA and hints at the Compass), the Compass after an emission in the springboards at the Dredge Station (sell it to Owl or keep it; keeping it reveals anomalies), the chimera hunt in Izumrudnoye (Zveroboy, at night), Nimble's special orders (SEVA, Vintorez, exoskeleton), the burnt-out Skat-5 in the Swamp (optional, gives the third crash its scene), and 18 stashes whose coordinates come from corpses' PDAs or from Owl.

## Level design

The map follows the layout of the original Zaton (the in-game satellite map and the wiki descriptions): the old riverbed with its marsh network runs from the Boiler in the north-east past Skadovsk and Shevchenko to the river that leaves the map in the east under the Preobrazhensky bridge; the Northern lake, the Swamp and the Izumrudnoye marsh lie in the north and west; the hills of the rim close the basin. 1 m of the world equals 2 px of the 2048 px original map, so the level is about 0.65 of the original size.

- **Places (west to east).** Sawmill (north-west: workshop with a log shed, the office with an attic and a ladder, the water-tower building, a pump house where the pipeline starts, barbed wire), Swamp with Skat-5 and a culvert shelter, Krug antenna complex (low building with a roof antenna inside a ring of masts, bloodsucker lair), Izumrudnoye pioneer camp on a hill (stage with an arched canopy, cabins, gate), Oakpine over a hollow of acid anomalies, Substation Workshops and the Iron Forest switchyard (south-west: Tesak's office, halls, merc tower, gantries and transformers, the control room with the transmitter, Skat-2 on the wires, the 42 m mast with a beacon, the railway spur into the tunnel to Jupiter), Burnt Farmstead on a hill (burnt log houses, one with an attic stash, a cellar, the plateau teleport), Northern lake with a boat pier, fuel station with a crack and a sunken car, the Claw, the Circus bowl, the concrete plant, Southern Plateau with Skat-3 and a snork cave, Shevchenko broken in two, Skadovsk on a sandbank, Old Barge with the Stoyanka quay, Port cranes with the house and containers, the Boiler island cut by ankle-deep cracks, the Dredge Station in a lake with a floating walkway, the Forestry compound, the Preobrazhensky bridge with the stopped military convoy, the Scar trench and the Waste Processing Station with its settling pond, drain and outfall pipes.
- **Pipeline.** One pipe runs across the whole map like in the original: from the Sawmill past the Swamp, over the creek and the roads (it arches over every road), down the hill to go underground under the old riverbed near Skadovsk, up again past the cranes, along the Forestry and onto the bridge, where it runs along the western edge and spans the collapsed part of the deck, then down to the station. It is a low obstacle the player can jump onto and walk along; AI walks under the arches.
- **Metrics.** Eye height 1.65 m, radius 0.32, step 0.55, doors ≥ 1.6 m, stairs rise ≤ 0.46 per metre. Water deeper than 1.3 m cannot be crossed. The marsh of the riverbed is shallow and slows the player down; only the lakes and the eastern river are deep, so the bridge (direct but full of springboards) and the shallow marsh west of the Stoyanka (long but safe) are the two ways across the river.
- **Landmarks.** Blinking red beacons on the Skadovsk mast and the Iron Forest mast stay visible up to 900 m, beyond the fog. The dredge with its ladder crane, the port cranes, the sawmill water tower, the Krug mast ring, the station buildings and the plateau cliffs sit on the skyline.
- **Tutorial slope.** From the eastern hill the player walks down a trail past a Zharka with a dead stalker (teaches bolts), a Firefly a few metres off the trail (teaches the detector) and flesh at the bus wreck (teaches combat). The trail meets the road from the cranes and leads to the Skadovsk gangway.
- **Hub.** Skadovsk is the safe zone: weapons are lowered, and it holds traders, the technician, a bed and shelter.
- **Shelters.** 40+ indoor zones double as emission shelters: ship holds, halls, cabins, the culvert, the snork cave and the tunnel. The HUD shows the distance to the nearest one during an emission.
- **Weather.** Fog cuts how far enemies see the player by up to half and rain by a fifth; rain also muffles footsteps by 40%. Fog is the stealth weather and the mutant-ambush weather at the same time. Fog is three times more likely in the morning. Beacons and lamps are drawn after the fog, so landmarks stay visible in it.
- **Risk and reward.** The best stashes (lair backpack with a Vintorez, crane cabin with an SVD and a Night Star, the plateau cave, the fuel station roof reached by a jump from the fuel tank) sit behind the biggest optional risks.
- **Saves.** Saves carry `v: 2`. A save from the old map keeps flags, quests, inventory, money, stash state and relations, rebuilds the population on the new map, moves the player into the Skadovsk bar and restores the Lotsman, the looter's corpse, Nimble's case and the Compass where the story needs them.

## Controls

WASD move, mouse or arrow keys look, LMB/Enter fire, RMB aim, Shift sprint, Ctrl/C crouch, Space jump, R reload or clear a jam, 1–4 weapons (press the active slot again to holster), H holster or draw the weapon, G grenade, B bolt, O detector, L flashlight, V fire mode, Y ammo type, F use, I/Tab inventory, P/M PDA, F1–F4 quick slots, F5/F9 quick save/load, F7 spawner, Esc menu.

## Interface

The interface follows the layout of the original game's screens, drawn as 1999 bitmaps: metal frames, hex mesh backgrounds and diamond plate are generated at start-up (`cp-skin`), so the game ships no image files.

- **HUD.** Round minimap on the satellite map with a bolted bezel, a north marker and the tracked objective on the rim. Under it are the clock and the tracked objective with its distance. Bottom left: message feed with a pictogram and time per line; PDA messages show the sender's portrait. Quick slots F1–F4 show item icons and counts. Bottom right: segmented health and stamina bars, magazine and reserve counts, fire mode and ammo icon. Status pictograms (bleeding, radiation, hunger, overload, psy, drunk, safe zone) stack above that panel.
- **Inventory (I/Tab).** The world stays visible on the left. The centre column holds the paper doll (pistol and rifle upright in tall slots, knife, suit, detector), quick slots, five artifact containers (unavailable ones are plated shut), the health bar and protection bars. The right column is the backpack: items occupy cells by size (a rifle is 5×2) and pack automatically by type. Hover shows an item card with bars and numbers; double-click uses or equips; right-click opens actions; drag an item onto a slot, the belt, a quick slot, back into the backpack, or onto the world area to drop it.
- **Trade.** A deal is assembled before anything changes hands. Each side has an offer tray above its grid: what Degtyarev sells (left) and what he buys (right), each with its total. Double-click moves one unit (a box of ammo, one piece of a stack, or a whole item) into the tray or back; dragging moves the whole stack; the context menu offers both with prices. The bar at the bottom shows the money after the deal or the shortfall. **Торговать** settles everything at once (sales first), and **Вернуть всё** empties both trays. Items the trader refuses are greyed out.
- **Loot.** Corpses and stashes work both ways: take items, or put items from the backpack into them (story items stay with the player). A corpse that holds the player's items is never cleaned up, and a stash stays in the world after it is emptied, so it can be used as storage. Mutant corpses show the mutant's portrait.
- **Upgrades.** Cardan's screen shows his portrait, the selected weapon or suit with a repair button, a three-tier upgrade chart (installed, locked, affordable) and the paper doll and backpack for picking what to work on.
- **Dialogue.** Portrait and name of the speaker, a scrolling transcript (the speaker in amber, Degtyarev in green), numbered replies (keys 1–9).
- **PDA (P/M).** Tabs for map, tasks, messages, encyclopedia (people and mutants with their portraits) and statistics, with the tracked task at the top. The map is a satellite view built from the terrain (hillshade, water, roads, tree crowns, roofs) with zoom, drag, filters (tasks, stashes, places, important people, shelters) and hover cards. Any active task can be tracked; the HUD follows it.
- **Characters.** Named characters look like their originals: Beard (bald, full dark beard, grey armour plates), Owl (bald, tan leather coat), Cardan (knit cap, moustache, Sunrise suit), Sultan (bald, moustache, dark leather coat), Noah (short fair hair, ginger beard, coat), Nimble (hood and face mask, light leather jacket), Tesak (exoskeleton with gas mask and helmet), Zveroboy and Lotsman (Sunrise suits), Degtyarev (Sunrise suit, hood down, chest rig).

## Spawner (F7)

F7, or **Спавнер (F7)** in the pause menu, opens a panel docked to the right edge. The world stays visible on the left, so anything created in front of the player shows up at once. The game is paused while the panel is open. F7 or Esc closes it.

- **Предметы.** Every entry of `DATA.ITEMS`: weapons (with the F-1 grenade), ammo, armour, detectors, artifacts, medicine and food, mutant parts and story items. Weapons and suits arrive new, with condition 100 % and a full magazine. Ammo is counted in boxes, and a firearm offers a one-click "+3 boxes" of its calibre. Items go to the backpack or onto the ground in front of the player. An artifact put on the ground becomes a glowing world artifact that is picked up with F. Story items can skip quest steps: the looter's PDA, for example, fires its story event.
- **Мир.** Every `NPC_TYPES` entry (6 human types, 5 mutants) and every anomaly type. Humans appear 5 m ahead and mutants 8 m ahead, spread in an arc and facing the player. Behaviour: *как обычно* (faction relations), *враждебно* (attacks at once), *дружелюбно* (never attacks the player and fights anything hostile to the player) or *спутник* (friendly and follows the player). Humans can be given any firearm. Anomalies are placed beyond their own trigger radius, so a Vortex does not pull the player in.
- **Погода.** Four tiles, each a live render of Zaton under that weather made by the same shaders. **Плавно** blends the sky, rain and fog over about a minute; **Сразу** switches at once and wets the ground for rain. **Держать** freezes the current weather so it does not change by itself; **Меняется сама** lets it change again. The card shows cover, wind, daytime visibility, how well enemies see and hear the player, and the current state (rain, fog, wet ground).
- **Placement.** `Game.spawnSpot` casts a ray along the view and stops short of walls. It keeps the player's floor level (the bar, ship decks) and skips deep water. If the view is blocked, it tries directions to the sides and behind.
- **Persistence and cleanup.** Spawned actors and ground items carry `spawned: true`; world artifacts and anomalies carry `field: 'spawned'`. They survive save/load (anomalies through `placedAnomalies`) and emissions. The **Убрать созданное** button removes all of them.

## Testing and verification

`tests/cop98.test.js` covers the following:
- World invariants: markers, a shelter near every place that promises one, reachability of every POI on the nav grid from the Skadovsk gangway, the plateau with no way up on foot but a path from its teleport to Skat-3, the bridge (deck walkable, west half collapsed, east half passable, river below), water coverage and deep water only in lakes and the river, the dredge in a deep lake and the Boiler island cut by shallow cracks.
- Sprite, view-model and sound coverage; an icon and a ground sprite for every item, backpack footprints and packing without overlaps, a portrait scene for every look and every mutant kind, sliced portrait rendering, double-resolution close-up sprites, and first-person hands on the grip of every weapon that shares its model with the icon.
- Renderer smoke test.
- The full scripted story (win, and an epilogue that reflects the choices made), including both plateau teleports.
- All four recorder branches, including stealth seen and unseen.
- Tools and upgrades, trade and consumables, deals with both trays (partial stacks, refused goods, shortfall), storing items in stashes (stack merge, story items refused, taking back), Noah, the Compass and the chimera, Lotsman (rescue, follow, save/load roundtrip).
- Emissions per difficulty, combat (dog, bandit, provoking a stalker), every dialogue node, and walk-through routes that climb the ship stairs, enter the interiors (Skadovsk, Shevchenko, Dredge Station by its walkway, Old Barge from the Stoyanka, Krug, Substation office, tunnel, sawmill workshop and office attic, Izumrudnoye cabin) and jump from the fuel tank onto the fuel station roof.
- Saves: the map version, a round trip, and the migration of an old-map save.
- The spawner: catalog coverage, backpack and ground spawns of every item, every creature type, behaviours and weapon choice, floor-level placement in the bar and on the Skadovsk deck, anomalies beyond their pull, save/load, emissions, cleanup, and companions that follow the player and kill a mutant and a hostile bandit.
- Weather: presets, smooth transitions (clouds before rain, drying ground), hold, automatic changes, thunderstorms, fog blinding an enemy at 50 m, save/load and old-save migration. Shaders: opaque frames in every weather, darker rain sky, cloud structure, stars on a clear night and none under rain clouds, rain streaks, darker wet ground, fog swallowing distant ground, lightning on clouds, tracers, muzzle flashes, point lights, electra arcs and colour grading.
- A zero-comments scan of every module.

For visual checks, drive headless Edge over CDP against `cop98.html`:
1. Call `cpui.newGame(1)`, then `cpui.closePanel(); cpui.mode = 'play'`.
2. Teleport by setting `cpui.game.player`.
3. Open the spawner with `cpui.openSpawner('')` (or dispatch F7) and click its buttons through `document.querySelector`.
4. Open screens with `cpui.openInventory()`, `cpui.openTrade(cpui.game.actorById('beard'))`, `cpui.openPDA('map')`. Headless Edge may not run requestAnimationFrame between commands: pump frames by calling `cpui.loop(t)` with `t` advancing from `cpui.last`, and stub `requestAnimationFrame` while pumping, otherwise every call starts another loop.
