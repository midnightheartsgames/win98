# Forza Horizon: Rīga

An open-world racing festival built from scratch for this project, imagined as a PC game from 2000. The Horizon festival comes to Riga in the summer of 2001: one seamless city with the Old Town, the Art Nouveau centre, the Daugava bridges, the port, Mežaparks, Biķernieki, the Spilve airfield, the A10 highway and the Jūrmala beaches. The career starts in a Lada 2105 and ends with a finale against Vilks in a Lamborghini Diablo SV. The renderer is WebGL 1 with three quality presets (Settings → Графика → Качество графики): **Ретро 2000** draws straight to the screen with dim highlights and no night lights, **Высокое** adds the post-processing chain, and **Ультра** (the default) adds sun shadows. The resolution and the 16-bit dithering are separate settings. The HUD is drawn on a 2D canvas, the menus are DOM, and engine sounds, effects and three radio stations are synthesised with Web Audio. All text is in Russian.

- Distribution: sold in the in-system Steam client (`steam: true` in `js/game-manager.js`, $46.99). The game is not preinstalled.
- After installation: desktop icon **Forza Horizon**, Start menu → **Forza Horizon: Рига**, `C:\Games\ForzaHorizon\FORZA.EXE` (with `README.TXT`), Run: `forza`, `forza.exe`, `fh`, `horizon`, `форза`, `рига`. Task Manager shows `forza.exe`.
- Window: `appForza.open()` in `js/apps/forza-app.js`, window id `app_forza`.
- Standalone page: `forza.html` (`window.fzui` is the UI instance, `fzui.game` the running career).
- The profile is stored in `localStorage` under `fh_riga_profile_v1`.
- Test: `npm run test:forza` (about 1970 checks in about 30 seconds, most of it the driver bot).
- Map data: the streets, parks and water of central Riga come from OpenStreetMap, © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL (credited in the game's `README.TXT`).
- Reference screenshots: `node tools/forza-shots.cjs [outDir] [scenes]` (see Tests).

## Controls

| Input | Action |
| --- | --- |
| W / Up | Throttle |
| S / Down | Brake, reverse when stopped |
| A, D / Left, Right | Steer |
| Space / Left Shift | Handbrake |
| Q / E | Gear down / up (manual gearbox) |
| R (hold) | Rewind |
| B (hold) | Look back |
| C | Camera: chase, far chase, hood, cockpit |
| Mouse (hold the left or right button and move) | Look around: from the cockpit the head turns up to 135° to the sides, 34° up and 40° down; from the hood up to 86°; the chase cameras fly round the car on a sphere (any angle round, from near the ground to almost straight above) |
| Mouse wheel | Chase camera farther or closer (0.7–1.8 of the normal distance) |
| Double click | Camera back to its place |
| V | Photo mode (see Content) |
| L | Headlights |
| H | Horn |
| Tab | Next radio station |
| Enter | Open the lobby of the event you are standing at |
| M | World map: waypoints, fast travel, event list |
| Backspace | Put the car back on the road (the tow truck also puts out an engine fire) |
| F (hold) | Patch the engine while the car stands still |
| Esc / P | Horizon menu in free roam, pause menu in a race |
| F7 | Debug panel: credits, XP and levels, damage, weather, time of day, teleport |

Mouse look: the eye turns round the neck like a real head, so it moves a few centimetres sideways and forward, and past about 60° it leans toward the shoulder it looks over; the rear-view mirror stays on the windscreen and leaves the screen when the head turns away. The chase camera keeps its distance and its smoothing while it orbits, stops at walls and never goes under the ground. B overrides the mouse look, switching the camera with C resets it. After the button is released the view returns behind the car or to the road (Settings → «Возврат камеры после обзора»: after half a second, after three seconds or never). Settings → «Камера и HUD» also has «Обзор мышью» (on or off), «Чувствительность мыши» (five steps, 26° per 100 px at the middle one) and «Мышь по вертикали» (normal or inverted). The cursor is hidden while the button is held; the middle button does nothing.

Menus use the arrow keys, Enter and Esc; Q and E switch tabs. Gamepads work through the Gamepad API (sticks and triggers for driving, A handbrake, B look back, Y rewind, D-pad right held to patch the engine, right stick click photo mode).

Near the camera a tree is a real model (a tapered trunk with bark, visible limbs, crown clusters lit as one volume with light coming through the leaves against the sun); farther away it becomes a painted three-plane impostor of the same tree, and the switch is a dithered crossfade at about 150 m instead of a pop. The ground is covered with grass tufts near the camera: mown lawns with dandelions and daisies in the city, tall meadow grass and lupins along the country roads, ferns, blueberries and heather in the forests, reeds with cattails at the water and marram grass on the dunes. The forests also get glacial boulders with moss and lichen, fallen trees, stumps, anthills and log piles along the forest roads; the meadows of Spilve and the outskirts get round straw and silage bales. Bushes and hedges are soft: the car bursts through them, loses a few km/h, the bush shakes and throws leaves, and a hit tree drops leaves from its crown. Boulders, logs, stumps and piles are solid; bales are smashable props that roll away and count for the wreckage skill. Race corridors, the railway and the festival grounds are kept clear, and solid objects stay at least 6 m off the carriageway. «Растительность» in the graphics settings switches between flat trees only, medium and high detail.
- **Weather:** five states — clear (with a varying amount of fair-weather clouds), overcast, downpour, thunderstorm and fog. In free roam the weather changes on its own every few minutes and blends over about a minute (cloud cover, rain, darkness, haze, wind); fog forms mostly around dawn. Fog is a ground layer with a height falloff: visibility drops to about 150 m, the sun turns into a pale disc with a glow around it, tall buildings and the TV tower rise out of the fog, and from a high camera the city lies under a white sea of fog with a clear sky above. The fog glows orange around the city lamps at night, street lamps and car lights get large halos, headlight beams become visible in the fog, all cars drive with their lights on, the roads stay damp, and a ship's foghorn sounds from the port now and then. Roads get wet during the rain (lower grip, puddles with rain ripples, spray behind the wheels, tyre hiss) and dry out over about two and a half minutes after it. Rain falls as streaks slanted by the wind and the car's speed, sheets of rain in the distance and splashes on the ground; a thunderstorm adds lightning bolts with branches, flashes that light the clouds and the whole scene, and thunder that arrives with the speed of sound. Events keep their own weather and the free-roam weather comes back afterwards. The weather is saved with the profile (old saves with «облачно»/«дождь» map to clear/downpour).
- **World:** traffic, day and night, the weather above, three radio stations. The weather tile on the main menu opens the time-of-day screen: a strip of the next 24 hours with the sky colours and the sun path, presets (dawn, morning, noon, sunset, night), ←/→ for 15 minutes, Q/E for an hour and mouse drag; the world behind the menu shows the chosen time live, Enter skips the clock forward (`Game.skipTime`, saved at once, not during an event) and Esc restores it. Traffic is spawned from the roads around the player only, so a road added elsewhere on the map does not change the traffic here. Traffic drivers keep to their lane, pick the lane for the next turn early, brake for bends and junctions and take each junction along a curve from their stop line into the target lane; the rear axle trails the front one, so the body turns smoothly and cuts the corner a little. Drivers from a smaller street stop and give way to the bigger road, everyone waits while a crossing path is taken by another car, the player or a rival, and nobody enters a junction whose exit is jammed. Stop lines are moved back until the car stands clear of the other roads, which matters at the acute forks. Cars follow the car ahead along their path (also through curves), and the body tilts with the road under its four wheels and dips, squats and leans under braking, acceleration and cornering. Every junction is laid out from its arms (`World.buildJunctions`): each corner between two neighbouring roads gets a rounded kerb whose radius equals the wider sidewalk, sidewalks and bridge parapets end where the kerb begins, an asphalt floor with the kerb curves covers the crossing and ends the lane markings, and any sidewalk piece left over the lanes of another road is cut away. Terrain is kept under the roads and sidewalks, so bridge embankments no longer rise through a neighbouring road.
- **Driving:** each wheel is simulated separately at 240 Hz. The sprung body has heave, pitch and roll on four suspension corners (spring, digressive damper, progressive bump rubber, anti-roll bars); full compression and full extension are inelastic limits, so curbs and steps never store energy and launch the car. Each wheel has its own mass, tyre stiffness and damping, load, spin and a combined-slip tyre curve with a friction ellipse. The drivetrain couples engine inertia to the driven axle through the clutch (slip at launch), an open or limited-slip differential or an AWD split, with engine braking and a rev limiter. Suspension, ride height, roll stiffness and differential depend on the car type and the suspension upgrade. Assists: ABS, traction control (keeps the driven wheels near peak slip), stability management, automatic or manual gears, standard or simulation steering, rewind.
- **Damage:** a log of impacts drives the damage of every car. A hit above about 8 km/h of closing speed dents the body where it struck: the depth follows the closing speed, the hardness of the obstacle (walls and lamp posts are hard, bins and fences soft) and the mass of the other car, the radius grows with the depth, and the dent gets a crumple noise, creased normals and darker scraped paint, adding up to 50 cm. Parts react on their own: a bumper comes loose, hangs from one end and drags sparks, then tears off; the hood unlatches, flaps in the wind, flips up at speed and blocks the view from the cockpit and the hood camera, then rips off; the trunk lid pops open and bounces; mirrors and rear wings break off. Torn parts fly away as physical debris painted like the car. The windscreen cracks into a spider web that is drawn on the glass from both sides and shatters after more hits; side and rear windows shatter into glass shards; headlights and tail lamps break and go dark, without their glow and without the headlight beam on that side. With full damage the car also drives differently: a hit at a wheel bends it, so the toe pulls the car to one side and the corner sags, and a hard one bursts the tyre (less grip, more rolling resistance, a lower corner and sparks from the rim). Engine damage depends on where the engine sits (front or rear by the weight distribution): the car loses up to half of its power, misfires and backfires, the smoke from the engine turns from white to black, and below 8 % the engine catches fire. After nine seconds of fire the car explodes, burns out (black paint, no glass, flat tyres, no lights) and a tow truck brings it back repaired after three seconds (CR 500 in free roam). Lost bumpers and an open or lost hood add drag, and a lost rear wing takes most of the downforce. Holding F (D-pad right) for three seconds at a standstill patches the engine up to 60 %, but not while it burns. Discovered outposts repair the car for free when you stop within 30 m, the Horizon menu sells a repair (the price grows with the damage and the car), every event starts with a fresh car and the free-roam damage comes back afterwards; damage is kept per car for the session. Rewind (R) rewinds the damage too, and torn parts that come back disappear from the road. Rivals, traffic and parked cars get dents, torn parts, cracked glass and broken lamps as well, and a battered rival loses up to 7 % of its pace. Settings → Повреждения: Полные (body and mechanics, the default), Только внешние (only the looks), Выключены. The HUD shows a small car silhouette next to the speedometer with the four zones, the engine and the wheels coloured by damage, the engine percentage, a fire countdown and the prompt for the field fix.
- **Destruction:** smashed props no longer vanish. The prop's own model is rebuilt from the primitives the city chunk drew (with the same random colours) and becomes one or more rigid bodies thrown by the car's momentum: lamp posts, park lamps, signs and traffic lights snap off and topple, bins, cones and crates fly whole, and benches, fence panels, stalls, kiosks, phone booths, tables and parasols break into up to four pieces at higher speed; city-light panels and phone booths shatter. The bodies tumble under gravity, bounce on the local slope of the ground with friction, slide, stop at walls, buildings and parked cars, get kicked by the player, rivals and traffic, sink in the water, fall asleep at rest, cast sun shadows and sink into the ground after about half a minute; the prop itself comes back later as before. XP boards and the stunt signs break the same way (the stunt signs used to stay standing). Up to 72 bodies live at once; wood, metal, glass and plastic knock differently.
- **Sky:** a sky model with a horizon glow toward the sun, a Mie halo and a limb-darkened sun disc; a cloud layer from a four-channel noise texture (base shapes, billowy detail, warp and cirrus) lit toward the sun with a silver lining at the edges, darker thick parts and rain shafts on the horizon; high cirrus in fair weather; moving cloud shadows on the ground; twinkling stars and a moon at night. Sun rays: the sky writes the cloud density and all solid geometry writes full occlusion into the alpha channel (transparent passes keep it), and a quarter-resolution post pass blurs the sunlit sky radially toward the sun in three steps, so the rays fall through gaps in the clouds, trees, buildings and the ferris wheel; they are strongest at a low sun. The lens flare is hidden when the sun is behind a building.
- **Graphics:** per-pixel sun and sky lighting, reflections on paint, glass and chrome, sun shadows for buildings, trees, props and cars, parked ones included (Ультра; the shadow map's texel grid is anchored near the camera, so shadows stay still while the sun moves and the camera drives, the edges are filtered over 4×4 texels, objects keep contact with their shadows down to wheels and parts lying on the road, leaves sway in the shadow map as they do on screen, and every car casts the shadow of the level of detail it is drawn with, dents and torn or hanging parts included), headlights and street lamps at night, lit windows, water with sky reflections and sun glitter, puddles on wet asphalt, bloom, radial speed blur, tone mapping with a soft shoulder, colour grading by time of day and a vignette. Cars are built from smoothed profiles with real wheel arches and wheel wells, wrap-around bumpers and three levels of detail picked by distance. Cars within 25 m have see-through tinted glass with rubber or chrome seals (drawn in a separate blended pass after all solid geometry and left out of the sun shadow map; farther cars draw dark opaque glass in the same call as the body; on Ретро 2000 the glass of near cars is dark and see-through), door pillars by the number of doors (black on newer foreign cars, body colour on Soviet and older cars), an interior silhouette seen through the windows (dash, steering wheel, seats with headrests, rear bench or bulkhead, headliner, parcel shelf), mirrors on stalks, door handles, wipers, an aerial, exhaust tips (single, split, quad or centre), fog lamps and plate frames. Pop-up headlights on the F355 and Diablo rise when the lights come on, and the Charger shows its hidden lamps behind the grille. Every car has its own procedural 512×512 skin with separate left and right sides, a top view and both ends: door and bonnet shut lines laid out from the real door and pillar positions, side mouldings, a fuel flap, text badges on the tail, ribbed lamp lenses, a grille pattern by type and its own Latvian number plate. The skin's alpha holds the paint mask and a second mask holds the livery's second colour, so the chosen paint colours stay live without redrawing; liveries are drawn into the skin at any level of detail. Skins for the traffic models are prepared on the loading screen; the others are made on first use (about 7 ms each). Reflections come from a small cube map of the current sky with the city skyline and the ground, redrawn when the hour or the weather changes, so paint, chrome, glass, water and wet asphalt reflect the real time of day. Paint is a clearcoat (Schlick Fresnel, a sharp and a broad highlight); the metallic finish adds a tinted blurry reflection and flakes that sparkle in the sun. Black rubber and plastic trim, lamp lenses, reverse lights and interiors have their own materials, the body has baked occlusion around the wheel arches, sills and undersides, every car within 150 m has a soft contact shadow that lies on the ground under its wheels, tilts with the slope and fades out when the car leaves the ground, lamp glows sit on each car's real lamps, the reverse lights come on in reverse, paint gets darker and glossier in the rain, and the player's car picks up dirt off road that rain and fords wash off (the paint shop cleans it). Wheels have tyre sidewalls, a tread that follows the tyre upgrade, eight rim styles (including the plastic wheel covers of the Mazda 626), brake discs and callipers (red on sports cars and with upgraded brakes) and turn into a blurred disc at speed. Cockpits have a dashboard shaped by the car's origin, the instrument cluster placed in the upper opening of the steering wheel, a closed interior (floor, door cards, pillars, headliner, sun visors, seats, rear bench or bulkhead) and a centre driving position in the McLaren F1. Instrument clusters follow the car's origin (Soviet, German, Japanese, American, Italian, 1950s) in nine layouts with a real odometer and clock, a boost gauge on turbo cars and warning lamps (ABS, traction control, handbrake, high beam, choke, shift light). Needles move on springs and sweep the dials at the start of an event, the dials glow in the car's backlight colour at night and the radio shows the station. In the cockpit the HUD keeps only the speed and gear. On Ultra the sun shines into the cabin through the windows and casts the shadows of the roof, pillars, seats and steering wheel (a separate small shadow map in the car's own space, redrawn only when the car turns against the sun or the steering wheel moves); looking into a low sun hazes the windscreen; in the rain (not on Ретро 2000) drops gather on every window, the wipers sweep in time with the drops they clear and leave the top of the screen wet; the rear-view mirror sits in a housing on a stalk. Other cars wipe too. Every car within 25 m has a driver (the player, rivals and traffic; parked cars and cars on show are empty, a burnt car has none): a low-poly figure on the driver's seat with a face painted into its own texture (skin tone, eyes, brows, lips, make-up, freckles, stubble), one of 16 hairstyles in one of 10 colours and a moustache, goatee or beard, dressed from a wardrobe of 67 items in nine slots (hats and helmets, glasses, tops and overalls, jackets, trousers and skirts, shoes, gloves, neckwear, watches and bracelets, with the festival wristband in the colour of the player's band; logos of the city's brands are printed on caps, T-shirts, jackets and overalls), whose hands hold the steering wheel and turn it hand over hand. The player's driver reaches for the gear lever on every shift with the manual gearbox, presses the horn, looks into turns, leans out in corners and forward under braking, bobs over bumps and on landings, is thrown toward the impact in a crash, glances at traffic after a near miss, turns round when the chase camera looks back, raises a fist after a win and drums on the wheel after 20 s standing still; rivals and traffic look into turns and lean too. Each of the 22 rivals has a look of their own (Вилкс drives the finale in a black leather jacket, mirrored glasses and racing gloves), Volga drivers in traffic are cab drivers in caps and leather jackets, other traffic drivers come from a pool of 32 looks built on the loading screen. In the cockpit the head and neck are not drawn, so the camera sees only the arms and hands; looking back from the cockpit (B, or turning the head with the mouse past 100°) hides the whole driver; the hood camera looking back shows the whole driver through the windscreen. The driver's head sits ahead of the B-pillar in every car. Rivals stand still in their grid slots during the countdown, leave the grid in their own columns and move onto the racing line over the first 70 m; they follow the line with speed planning, overtake the player, each other and traffic only when they would close in within 3 s, keep room to a car alongside instead of steering into it, never drive through a slower car in their path, collide with traffic and use rubber banding; five difficulty levels scale their pace and the showcase vehicles.
- **Participant registration:** «Новая игра» opens «Регистрация участника» before the prologue: the player's name (2–16 letters of any alphabet, digits, spaces and «_»; it appears on the top bar, the pass and the festival screens), figure, face, skin, eye colour, hairstyle and hair colour, and the starter T-shirt or tank top and jeans in their colours, with the standing driver in the studio. Everything starts filled at random, so one Enter is enough; «Случайно» rolls again, Esc returns to the title without creating a profile. A save made before drivers existed gets a look from the player's name and the starter set, and a one-time banner «Новое на фестивале: ваш водитель».
- **Driver tab:** the Horizon menu has a ВОДИТЕЛЬ tab between МАШИНЫ and НАВЫКИ with the participant pass (portrait rendered from the player's look, name, level, wristband colour, participant number, a champion stamp after the finale) and tiles for the appearance and the wardrobe. Both screens show the driver standing in a studio next to the current car (breathing, shifting weight, glancing around); the camera frames the head, the upper body or the whole figure depending on the selected row, Q/E and mouse drag turn the figure. Внешность: figure, face, skin, eye shape and colour, brows, hairstyle and colour, facial hair, make-up and freckles, each changed with ←/→ with a live preview, «Случайно» and «Сохранить»; Esc asks before dropping changes. Гардероб: nine slots and the festival wristband, owned items of the selected slot with their colours (←/→), a preview on focus, a note about overalls and helmets, «Надеть» saves. Образы keeps three saved outfits with a small portrait. The boutique costs about a third of a full career's income (CR 315,400 for 51 items). The F7 panel can fill the wardrobe with every item.
- **Photo mode:** V (the right stick click on a gamepad, or «Фоторежим» in the Horizon menu and in the race pause menu) freezes the world: traffic, rivals, particles, rain, lightning and the festival stop where they are. The camera orbits the car or flies free up to 60 m from it and 40 m up, and it never enters buildings, the ground or the car. Controls: mouse drag or WASD to orbit or fly, R/F up and down, the wheel for distance, Z/X or Shift+wheel to zoom, Tab switches the camera mode, the arrows change the settings, Q/E switch the tabs, H hides the interface, Space takes the photo, G opens the album, Esc returns to the game. The settings panel has five tabs. КАДР: camera mode, lens from 12 to 300 mm (35 mm film equivalent, shown with its field of view), horizon tilt, frame format (screen, 4:3, 3:2, 16:9, 1:1, 4:5, 2.39:1) and a viewfinder grid. ОПТИКА: aperture from f/1.4 to f/22 with real depth of field (the scene depth goes to a depth texture, and the blur size follows the focal length, the aperture and the focus distance), focus on the car or at a set distance, motion blur toward the car, vignetting and sun flares. ЦВЕТ: exposure in thirds of a stop, contrast, saturation, seven film looks (colour matrices) and grain. СЦЕНА: time of day and weather for the photo only, headlights, brake lights, wheel angle, and hiding your car or the other cars. ПЕЧАТЬ: an orange seven-segment date imprint like on a 2001 compact camera (the date follows the festival calendar from 14 July 2001, one day per finished event), a caption with the place and the car, the festival logo, an instant-photo or 35 mm film frame, and the size from 800×600 to 1600×1200. The viewfinder shows the frame format, the grid, the focus point and the print, and a readout with the lens, aperture, focus distance, exposure and place. A photo is rendered again at the chosen size and saved as a JPEG to `C:\My Documents\Мои рисунки\Forza Horizon\RIGA_0001.JPG` (it opens in Paint; if the disk is full, the game says so; the standalone page keeps photos for the session and offers a download). The album shows each photo large with a film strip of thumbnails, the place, car, time, weather, date and landmarks, and can set a route to where the photo was taken, make the photo the desktop wallpaper or delete it to the Recycle Bin. The photo hunt pays +750 XP and CR 1,000 for the first photo of each named landmark with your car in the frame (the landmark must be near enough, fill a noticeable part of the frame and not stand behind buildings) and CR 10,000 for all of them; the album lists the landmarks that are done, and the viewfinder says which ones are in the frame. The chosen look is saved in the profile. Photo mode works on all three graphics presets.
